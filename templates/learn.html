Feel. Look. Tilt.

Three checks that take ten seconds. If any of them feels wrong, treat the note as suspicious.

1. Feel2. Look3. Tilt
{{ icon('scan') }}

Paper and raised print

Genuine banknote paper feels crisp and different from normal paper. Some parts of the print are raised and can be felt with a fingertip.

{{ icon('eye') }}

Watermark

Hold the note up to the light. A faint image should appear and look smooth and detailed, not printed on the surface.

{{ icon('layers') }}

Security thread

A thin thread or strip runs through the paper. Against the light it should look like a continuous line.

{{ icon('zap') }}

Colour-shifting ink

On many notes, some numerals or patches change colour when you tilt the note.

{{ icon('image') }}

Fine detail

Genuine printing has sharp, tiny details. Blurry lines, smudges or flat colours are warning signs.

{{ icon('lock') }}

Serial numbers

Check the numbers are evenly spaced and aligned. Two notes with the same serial number cannot both be real.

Security features differ by country and denomination. Always follow your central bank's official guide.
